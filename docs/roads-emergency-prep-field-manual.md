# The Roads — Emergency Preparedness Field Manual

> ⚠️ **Read this first.** This manual is a *companion to hands-on training, not a
> substitute for it.* A kit you don't know how to use can be worse than no kit at
> all — an improperly applied tourniquet, an untrained airway insertion, or bad
> water treatment can hurt someone more than the emergency would have. Use this to
> **remember what you've already been trained to do**, and to build and check your
> kit. Get real first-aid and preparedness training (Stop the Bleed, CPR/AED, a
> first-aid course). When in doubt, the answer is: get training.

A plain-language distillation of the emergency-preparedness run on **The Roads with
Belle** (Beau of the Fifth Column). Checklists first; watch the linked video for the
reasoning and demonstration behind each one.

**Fidelity note:** Except where marked, sections were built from YouTube
**auto-captions**, which mis-hear names, numbers, and technical terms. Treat any
specific brand, quantity, ratio, or time as *approximate* — confirm against the
video and the product's own instructions before you rely on it. Safety-critical
numbers (boil times, disinfection ratios, tourniquet use) especially.

## Grab-and-go essentials (the "big six")

If you remember nothing else, cover these six needs. Each has a dedicated section below.

- **Water** — a filter (LifeStraw / Sawyer) **plus** chemical tablets (Aquatabs); ability to boil.
- **Fire & light** — carry *two+* ignition methods (metal lighter + ferro rod); "if you have one, you have none."
- **Food** — 2-week minimum of shelf-stable staples; rotate old-to-front.
- **Shelter** — tent with real rain fly + a tarp + Mylar blanket; insulate from the ground.
- **Knife** — one full-tang fixed blade (construction matters more than brand).
- **First aid** — a *trauma* IFAK (tourniquet, hemostatic gauze, chest seals) kept **separate** from your everyday boo-boo/meds kit — **and the training to use it.**

Keep important documents (birth certs, insurance, SS cards) digitized on a thumb drive.
Have a family communication and evacuation plan. Plan for a **2-week** minimum.

## First aid — the IFAK

### The Roads to Emergency First Aid with an IFAK (2026-08-27)
*Source: Whisper transcription (higher fidelity).* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E7U_d9_vNW4)

**Covers:** What an IFAK (Individual First Aid Kit) is — a compact **trauma** kit built
to keep someone alive until advanced care arrives. It handles what kills in minutes:
massive bleeding, certain airway problems, penetrating chest wounds. It is *not* your
under-the-sink first-aid kit.

**Core IFAK contents:**
- **Tourniquet** — for life-threatening bleeding from an arm/leg when direct pressure isn't enough. Buy quality; upgrade a generic kit's tourniquet first to a **CAT tourniquet (preferably Gen 7)**. Not for neck/chest/abdomen.
- **Hemostatic gauze** (e.g. QuikClot) — for severe bleeding where a tourniquet can't go (junctional wounds — groin, armpit). Pack it *into* the wound to pressure the bleeding source.
- **Compressed gauze** — plain gauze in a tiny package; wound packing, pressure, dressing, backup after the hemostatic gauze is used.
- **Pressure dressing** (Israeli/emergency trauma bandage) — maintains pressure so your hands are freed. Not a substitute for a tourniquet on catastrophic limb bleeding.
- **Chest seals** — for penetrating chest wounds; carried in **pairs** (entry + exit). **Vented preferred** over non-vented.
- **Trauma shears** — cut clothing away to see the injury; blunt tip avoids stabbing the patient.
- **Medical gloves** — *more than one pair* (they tear, get contaminated, and helpers need some).
- **Emergency (Mylar) blanket** — blood loss drives hypothermia, which worsens clotting → more bleeding. Prevent heat loss.
- **Medical tape** — secure dressings, write treatment notes (a 3" silk tape is a favorite).
- **Permanent marker** — record **tourniquet application time** (critical for saving the limb) and notes for the next provider.

**Advanced / training-gated (don't carry what you can't use):**
- **Nasopharyngeal airway (NPA / "nasal trumpet")** — training required; lube it.
- **Needle decompression** — for trained, authorized personnel treating tension pneumothorax **only**. Not a general first-aid technique; wrong placement injures vessels/nerves/lungs.

**Do / Don't:**
- Do: keep the trauma IFAK **separate** from your minor-injury/meds kit, and keep it *on you*.
- Do: **get training** — Stop the Bleed, CPR/AED, first aid. Practice on *training* gear, not the tourniquet you'll trust in a real event.
- Don't: buy mystery-brand imitations of proven trauma devices.
- Don't: use airway or decompression tools without proper training — "a pouch full of unfamiliar objects" saves no one.

## Water

### The Roads to Emergency Water Purification (2026-02-19)
*Source: auto-captions — fidelity limited.* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_kdTtVWjo5A)

**Covers:** Purification methods — portable filters, chemical disinfection, boiling.
Filters handle bacteria/parasites but **not viruses or chemicals**; chemical methods add coverage.

**Kit / checklist:**
- LifeStraw or LifeStraw Go cup — no setup, ~1,000 gallons.
- Sawyer filter + Smartwater bottles — needs setup, lasts tens of thousands of gallons.
- P&G Purifier of Water packets — one packet, shake 5 min, settle 30 min, ~10 L clean; ~3-yr shelf life.
- Aquatabs — one tablet ~2 L in 30 min; ~5-yr shelf life in foil.
- Forward-osmosis pouches (salt/brackish water) — ~450 ml over 4–8 hrs; 5–7 yr shelf life.
- Metal military canteen + mess kit/cup — lets you **boil** as a fallback.
- Coffee filter — pre-strain cloudy water before chemical treatment.

**Do / Don't:**
- Do: **boil at a full rolling boil ≥1 minute** (longer at higher elevation). *(confirm exact time from source)*
- Do: combine methods (filter + chemical) to also cover viruses.
- Don't: assume cool, clear, moving water is safe — Giardia/Cryptosporidium can be present.
- Don't: put standard plastic/green canteens directly on a fire.

## Fire & light

### The Roads to Emergency Fire and Light (2026-03-19)
*Source: auto-captions — fidelity limited.* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xopS9DmH-ik)

**Covers:** Fire's uses (cooking, purifying water, warmth, light, signaling) and multiple
backup ignition methods. "If you have one, you have none."

**Kit / checklist:**
- Metal lighter (Zippo/trench style) — refillable, works in cold.
- Bic lighter — cheap and abundant, but poor in cold.
- Waterproof matches — backup / to hand to another person.
- Ferro rod + striker — most reliable bushcraft method; scrape the coating off a new rod.
- Magnesium bar — scrape shavings onto tinder, then spark.
- Fresnel lens / parabolic starter — focus sunlight onto tinder (needs direct sun); parabolic doubles as a signal mirror.
- Chem lights / glow sticks — duration on the package; cold = dimmer + longer, warm = brighter + shorter.
- Flashlight/lantern (alkaline batteries; battery + foil is itself a fire method).
- Sterno tablets — heat and light.
- Cooking tripod + chain for a pot; a grater to make fine tinder.

**Do / Don't:**
- Do: carry multiple ignition methods; learn bow-and-drill but carry a lighter as primary.
- Don't: rely on a lens at dusk or in cold when sunlight is weak.

### The Roads to Understanding Fire (2024-04-04)
*Source: auto-captions — fidelity limited.* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CW9bOlaRDWI)

**Covers:** Fire from first principles (heat, fuel, oxygen), tinder/kindling, and common lays.

**Kit / checklist:**
- Tinder: dryer lint, wood shavings, dry pine straw, 0000 steel wool, deer moss.
- Kindling by size: pencil → thumb/finger → wrist.
- Tools: ferro rod + steel striker, battery + foil, matches.
- **Dakota fire hole:** dig a hole 6–8" wide, up to ~10" deep; a second hole ~12" away angled to meet the bottom (feeds air).
- **Log cabin lay:** big pieces on the bottom, smaller going up, tinder in the center; blow to sustain.
- **Teepee/lean-to lay:** larger pieces leaning together, thinner against them, opening away from the wind, tinder inside.

**Do / Don't:**
- Do: keep tinder fine; blow slowly at the base; keep extra kindling ready.
- Do: keep flammables ~10 ft away, firewood upwind, a shovel nearby; never leave a fire unattended.
- Don't: use unprocessed green limbs — dry split wood burns better. Don't assume you have your lighter.

## Food

### The Roads to Emergency food storage and preparation (2026-01-29)
*Source: auto-captions — fidelity limited.* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gOS_LfDaVEg)

**Covers:** Long-term food storage, from shelf-stable staples to commercial rations.

**Kit / checklist:**
- Never-spoil staples: honey (rewarm if crystallized), sugar (dry & sealed), salt, vinegar.
- Long-storing dry goods: beans, white rice, dry pasta, oats.
- **Mylar bags + oxygen absorbers in food-safe buckets** — the long-term-storage gold standard.
- Grocery low-acid canned foods (meats, beans, vegetables) — ~5 yrs to best-by.
- Commercial brands mentioned: Mountain House (taste/calories), Ready Harvest (veg/canned), Emergency Essentials (just-add-water bread), Nutrient Survival (nutrition data), ReadyWise (variety — watch shelf life on hiking meals). *(brand names via captions — verify)*

**Do / Don't:**
- Do: mix and match brands; rotate stock old-to-front.
- Don't: put oxygen absorbers with salt or sugar (they clump into bricks) or with damp/sticky foods (botulism risk).

## Shelter

### Roads to Understanding Emergency Shelters (2026-06-04)
*Source: auto-captions — fidelity limited.* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zt59HTkN6sA)

**Covers:** Choosing shelter by climate and resources — tents, tarps, found structures, improvised builds.

**Kit / checklist:**
- Tent with a **full** rain fly and bathtub-style waterproof floor.
- Tarp — roof, windbreak, shade, water collection, repair. "Just carry a tarp."
- Mylar blankets / tents — lighter than tarps, less durable.
- Cold: thick bedding of leaves, pine needles, grasses, evergreen branches — **insulate from the ground first.**
- Snow: snow itself insulates; a snow cave traps warm air.
- Tropical: layered palm leaves, tarp angled steeply for runoff.
- Arid: prioritize overhead **shade** over full enclosure.

**Do / Don't:**
- Do: identify the primary threat (cold/heat/wind/rain) and build against it.
- Do: inspect found structures for weak walls/roofs, glass, electrical hazards, mold, bad water.
- Don't: assume a lightweight backpacking tent survives a real winter.
- Don't: fully enclose shelter in tropical/arid heat — it traps heat and kills airflow.

## Knife

### The Roads to choosing an emergency knife (2026-04-30)
*Source: auto-captions — fidelity limited.* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N9WBERuqVRs)

**Covers:** Fixed blade, pocket, machete, hatchet — and the one feature that matters: **full tang.**

**Kit / checklist:**
- **Fixed blade, full tang** (metal runs blade-through-handle to the butt).
- Proven picks: Ka-Bar, Gerber, Air Force Survival Knife.
- Budget: **Mora Companion** (reliable, rust-resistant); **Camillus** (knife/machete/hatchet/multi-tool sets, ~$30 during holidays).
- Pocket knife with clip + one-hand opening (Spyderco, Gerber, Kershaw, Benchmade).
- Machete with a long tang; hatchet with as long a handle as you'll carry.
- Multi-tool (plier or Swiss-Army style); paracord-handled kits (<$40) add usable cordage.

**Do / Don't:**
- Do: prioritize construction (full tang) over brand; start budget if cost is a barrier.
- Don't: dismiss paracord handles — the cord itself is survival-useful.

## Go-bags & vehicle kits

### The Roads to an inexpensive emergency bag (2025-03-13)
*Source: auto-captions — fidelity limited.* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1o2--ECQJVs)

**Covers:** A surprisingly complete **~$40** survival kit in a compact IFAK-style pouch, for people on a budget.

**Kit / checklist (as shown):**
- 8×5 Mylar tent + string; Mylar blanket; thin rain poncho.
- Small fishing kit + pre-treated line; rope / paracord bracelet (snares).
- Scrape fire starter + a striker built into the bracelet.
- Pocket flashlight with strobe; tiny lantern/flashlight; chem lights.
- Tiny folding e-tool (shovel); ring hand-saw.
- First aid: band-aids, tweezers, small scissors, safety pins, triangular bandage, gauze, prep pads, tape, gloves, cotton swabs.
- Pocket knife + tactical spork; multitool (hammer/knife/pliers/wire cutters).
- Whistle, compass, pen with window-breaker point; can/bottle opener card; phone pouch + water-bottle clip.

**Do / Don't:**
- Do: **add water treatment** (LifeStraw/Sawyer/tablets) — the kit has everything *but* water; upgrade components over time.
- Don't: expect high-grade gear — these are survival basics, not backcountry equipment.

### The Roads to safety on the road — opening a 5-year-old survival kit (2022-01-12)
*Source: auto-captions — fidelity limited.* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JASVnNrCKmQ)

**Covers:** What to pack in a **vehicle** kit — demonstrated by opening a 5-yr-old kit that had mostly failed. Annual checks matter.

**Kit / checklist:**
- First aid kit; Mylar blanket; N95 + gloves.
- Multiple flashlights + chem lights; can opener; multi-tool + knife; hatchet.
- Paracord bracelets (some with fire striker); compass; duct tape; toilet paper.
- Tire plug kit; folding stove + candle/biofuel; siphon (emergency fuel transfer).
- Batteries + lighter/matches (check yearly — corrosion & moisture ruin them).
- Tampons w/o applicator (fluff = excellent kindling); fishing line (fishing/snares).
- Water bottles; food: rice, canned (pork & beans), hard biscuits.

**Do / Don't:**
- Do: **check and rotate contents every year**; tailor to your climate and skills.
- Don't: assume a kit still works after months baking in a hot car.

## Disaster response & Q&A

### The Roads to Disaster Preparedness (2024-01-11)
*Source: auto-captions — fidelity limited.* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MM-Qol7wzv4)

**Covers:** Response for tornado, hurricane, earthquake, flood, blizzard + a core kit.

- **Tornado:** *Watch* = possible, *Warning* = sighted. Basement/cellar or small interior room, no windows. Outside → low flat ground (**not** under a bridge/overpass); cover head & neck.
- **Hurricane:** evacuate. Most injuries come **after** the storm. Never enter flood water (sewage, bacteria, hidden debris). Generators **outdoors only**. Don't run a chainsaw untrained.
- **Earthquake:** prep the home ahead; get under sturdy furniture; know how to shut off gas/water/electric.
- **Flood:** high ground; if trapped in an attic, make an exit hole **before** water rises.
- **Blizzard:** heat one small room (body heat + candles used safely), door closed.
- **Core kit:** food, water (+ purification), fire/light, shelter (tent/Mylar), a good first-aid kit *you're trained on*, 1–2 weeks of essential meds, knife, document copies on a thumb drive, family comms/evac plan.

### The roads to emergency preparedness questions (2023-07-02)
*Source: auto-captions — fidelity limited.* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O0_K1MT3JxI)

**Covers:** Q&A on essentials, persuading skeptics, and why knowledge beats gear.

**Kit / checklist:**
- Food: survival ration bricks (heat-resistant, ~5-yr, taste poor); canned survival water (survives high heat).
- Fire: flashlights, lighters, matches; tampons as kindling.
- Shelter: Mylar blanket. First aid: tourniquet, chest seals, shears, airway tape.
- Knife: compact folder. Add-ons: rubber bands, super glue, WD-40.

**Do / Don't:**
- Do: prioritize **knowledge** — download free military field manuals from archive.org; digitize key papers to a thumb drive.
- Don't: pitch prep head-on to skeptics — frame it around their interests (even "zombies").

### The roads to preparedness questions (2022-07-14)
*Source: auto-captions — fidelity limited.* · [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=obqLU7c6QbI)

**Covers:** What people forget, essential training, storage minimums vs. overkill.

**Kit / checklist:**
- Papers (birth cert, insurance, SS) on a thumb drive; pocket-sized EDC of the big six.
- **Training:** Wilderness First Responder (~$500) or any good first-aid cert covering tourniquets/bleeding control.
- Power: car battery + jumper cables + USB adapter, or a $20–40 inverter.
- Food: canned is cheaper than MREs — rotate old-to-front. Flood: cheap inflatable rafts.
- Morale: cards, travel games, coloring books.

**Do / Don't:**
- Do: plan a **2-week minimum**; use "zombie apocalypse" framing to persuade friends.
- Don't: let prep become an all-consuming identity; don't help *inside* a disaster zone untrained — offer skills at relief centers instead.
